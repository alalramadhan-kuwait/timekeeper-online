## The approach: real faces, paper bodies

Likeness is the biggest risk. A generated face can drift from the real person scene to scene. So the plan is a **hybrid**:

1. **Head: cut from a real photograph.** The founder's own photo, cut out, given a thin white paper edge and a paper shadow. Likeness is guaranteed because it is the person. This gives "a photograph cut from paper and brought to life".
2. **Body, arms, legs: generated paper-craft pieces** in the Time Keeper paper style, with period-appropriate clothing, built to the rig sizes in `../CHARACTERS.md`.
3. **Fallback:** if a photo head looks wrong on a paper body, the whole figure is generated from the photo as one paper-cut character and checked against the photo.

I will test option 1 on one founder first (Gate 3 starts with one, not three) and show you before doing the rest.

## Consistency rules once locked

- One reference image per character is attached to every generation. No prompt describes the face from scratch.
- The same paper texture, edge width and shadow for every figure.
- A likeness check against the photograph before any asset enters a scene: face shape, beard, hair line, skin tone, glasses if worn, proportions.
- Any asset that fails is discarded, never repaired scene by scene.

## Risks

- **Likeness drift** is why heads come from the real photo.
- **Photo quality.** Small or blurry photos cannot be cut cleanly. I need sharp, evenly lit, front-facing images.
- **Consent.** Every founder must agree to be shown as a paper character. This is a precondition, not a formality.
- **Cost.** Roughly 30 to 40 images across six characters including revisions, plus background removal for the parts. I will quote a single trial before spending more.
